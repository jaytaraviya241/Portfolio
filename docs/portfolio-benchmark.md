# Shopify portfolio benchmark

Research date: 30 August 2026

## Design direction

The portfolio will keep its established near-black, warm-paper, and amber identity, but move from a sparse project archive to a proof-led editorial system. The central idea is **measured momentum**: expressive enough to feel premium, structured enough to show how each storefront moves a business, customer journey, or merchant workflow forward.

The homepage hierarchy is: positioning → verifiable archive proof → capabilities → selected work → working point of view → project invitation. Project pages retain the detailed case-study format and make the challenge, role, decisions, delivered system, evidence limits, outcomes, and lessons easy to scan.

## Recurring content strategies

1. **Specific category and buyer language.** The strongest sites say which teams, platforms, and commercial situations they are for.
2. **Problem-first project framing.** The client logo is not the story. The useful story is the constraint, the decision, and what became possible afterward.
3. **Proof near the claim.** Metrics, platform facts, screenshots, quotes, or a transparent evidence note appear beside the relevant statement.
4. **A visible point of view.** Premium portfolios explain what they believe about conversion, maintainability, performance, and the client relationship.
5. **Merchant outcomes matter.** Content control, operational simplicity, and launch confidence are treated as product outcomes alongside customer-facing UX.
6. **Clear engagement models.** Buyers can quickly recognize whether they need a full build, implementation partner, or focused improvement.
7. **The CTA reflects the buyer's stage.** “Discuss a similar build” and “Bring the constraint” are more relevant than a generic contact link.

## Messaging framework for Jay Taraviya

- **Audience:** DTC founders, ecommerce leads, designers, and agencies that need an accountable Shopify implementation partner.
- **Value proposition:** Design-faithful Shopify storefronts that improve decision clarity for customers and remain practical for the merchant team to operate.
- **Differentiators:** A documented archive across three engagement models; responsive translation from design to real commerce content; and evidence-aware case studies that do not invent private metrics.
- **Proof strategy:** Use the supplied storefront captures, live links, project types, and documented decisions. Reserve placeholders for client-approved metrics, testimonials, timelines, and team details.
- **Voice:** Direct, calm, technically credible, commercially aware, and free of inflated performance claims.

## Content guardrails

- Do not claim revenue, conversion, speed, accessibility compliance, team size, project duration, or client sentiment without supplied evidence.
- Do not present a live brand's current interface as proof that every visible feature was implemented by Jay.
- Clearly distinguish shipped archive evidence from professional interpretation.
- Keep copy specific to Shopify storefront systems, customer decision paths, responsive delivery, and merchant operation.
